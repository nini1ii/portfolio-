Project 1

PTOG Financial Analysis & Investment Advisory

DEC LASU Hackathon | Group 1 (Data Haven), Team Lead | May 2026 | Excel, PowerPoint, Word

A private equity fund was considering a stake in Pepper Them Oil & Gas Ltd. (PTOG), a Nigerian oil and gas company. Our team audited an uncleaned five-year dataset (FY2019 to FY2023), rebuilt the financial statements, analysed performance and made an investment recommendation.

What I did

  • Led a team of four through the data audit, reconstruction and reporting.
  • Audited six raw datasets and logged 22 issues: duplicates, NGN/USD mix-ups, missing values, misclassified items, mixed date formats and unit errors (11 resolved, 4 corrected, 3 estimated, 2 reclassified, 2 flagged).
  • Estimated the missing FY2023 gas processing revenue at $79.4M using the average gas-to-oil revenue ratio (about 11.4%) and labelled it as an estimate.
  • Reclassified interest received from investing to operating cash flow (IFRS) and flagged gaps the source data could not resolve rather than forcing them to balance.
  • Rebuilt the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement, calculated ratios across six areas, and presented an advisory deck and written report.
$827.7MFY2023 revenue (from $371.7M in FY2020)
41.7%FY2023 EBITDA margin
11.8×Interest coverage, FY2023
$81.1MFY2023 free cash flow

Recommendation

Cautious Accumulate. Buy if Brent holds above $70/bbl and the auditors resolve the balance sheet gaps; hold if oil sits between $55 and $70 with a credible management explanation; review if the gaps widen or refinancing risk appears.

Limitation. Equity-based ratios use our corrected retained earnings, which leave the balance sheet check off in FY2020 to FY2023. If equity is anchored to assets minus liabilities, FY2023 debt-to-equity is about 0.60× rather than 0.46×. The deleveraging trend and the recommendation still hold.

Project 2

Bootcamp Financial Model & Competitive Analysis

Datacraft Hub | Data Analytics Internship | September 2026 | Excel, Word, PowerPoint

Datacraft Hub planned a free, three-month weekend Data Analytics Bootcamp for 50 participants. Management needed to know the cost, whether the financial model could be trusted, how it compared with competitors, and how sponsors could fund it.

What I did

  • Built a cohort financial model and presentation: 15 facilitators at ₦50,000 each, marketing ₦70,000, administration ₦50,000 and materials ₦25,000 per participant.
  • Audited the workbook and found integrity issues: currency labelled "$" while values were in naira, durations that did not match the three-month programme, a per-participant cost that did not scale with enrolment, and a "Processing Fees" line carrying the ₦1.5M expected revenue figure, which pushed total cost to ₦2,395,000.
  • Benchmarked four Nigerian training providers on price, duration and inclusions, mapped a ₦4.1M sponsorship pipeline, and wrote a report with recommendations and KPIs.

Key findings

  • A free programme sits below paid competitors' published prices (₦75,000 to ₦150,000 and up), so sustainability depends on sponsorship.
  • Competitors bundle projects, mentorship and job-readiness support, so Datacraft should compete on visible outcomes, not price alone.
  • Correct the model before management relies on it, and treat the ₦4.1M as a pipeline target, not guaranteed income.

Retail Sales Analysis (Excel Graded Assessment)

DEC LASU Data Analytics Boot Camp | 2026 | Excel

Analysed 12 retail orders across four cities and three product categories to explain what drives sales.

What I did

Sales are highly concentrated. Only 3 of the 12 orders were tagged high performance (profit above ₦30,000), yet they produced about 65% of both sales and profit.
